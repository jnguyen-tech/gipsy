# Reading a price chart

## Candlesticks
Each candle shows four prices for one period: open, high, low and close. The thick part (the body) runs from open to close. On TradingView's default colours it is green when the close is above the open and red when it is below. The thin lines (wicks or shadows) reach the high and the low. A long upper wick means buyers pushed price up during the period but sellers pushed it back down before the close. A long lower wick means the opposite. A tiny body with long wicks on both sides (a doji) shows indecision. Single candles mean little on their own. They matter more at an important level, on a higher timeframe, and with high volume.

## Trend: higher highs and higher lows
An uptrend is a series of higher highs and higher lows: each bounce starts from a higher floor. A downtrend is lower highs and lower lows. A range (sideways market) keeps bouncing between roughly the same high and low. The first sign a trend may be ending is a broken pattern, for example an uptrend that makes a lower low. Moving averages help you see trend direction at a glance: price above a rising 50 or 200 EMA is generally an uptrend. Most beginner losses come from fighting the trend because something "looks too high" or "must bounce".

## Support and resistance
Support is a price zone where buying has stopped falls before. Resistance is a zone where selling has stopped rises before. They are zones, not exact lines, so draw them through clusters of wicks and closes. The more times a level has held, and the more visible it is (a round number, a previous all-time high, a yearly low), the more people watch it. When a level finally breaks, it often flips role: old resistance becomes new support, and the other way round. A breakout that quickly falls back below the level is called a failed breakout or fakeout.

## Volume
Volume is how much traded during each candle. It is the bars at the bottom of the chart. Moves on rising volume show conviction. Moves on shrinking volume are easier to reverse. A breakout through resistance on heavy volume is more trustworthy than one on light volume. Crypto volume differs by exchange, and spot forex has no central volume at all, so volume is most useful on stocks, ETFs and futures.

## Timeframes and noise
The same asset can be in an uptrend on the weekly chart and a downtrend on the hourly chart at the same time. Neither is wrong. They describe different time horizons. Pick the timeframe that matches how long you plan to hold something. Longer-term investors live on daily and weekly charts. Day traders use minutes. A beginner learns faster on daily charts, because each candle is a full day of decisions and there is less random noise.

## Log scale vs linear scale
On a linear scale, every $10 move takes the same vertical space. On a log scale, every 10% move takes the same space. For assets that have grown a lot, such as bitcoin or a stock over many years, log scale is the honest view: a move from $1 to $2 and from $50,000 to $100,000 are both a doubling and look the same size. In TradingView, toggle log scale with the small "log" button at the bottom right of the price axis, or press Alt+L.
